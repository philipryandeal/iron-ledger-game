# The first case — The Room That Forgot Its Name

*The opening every Keeper plays first. It teaches the two columns, finite ink, and what it costs to remember.*

## The room

A small side room off the forest — a nursery that never held a child, or a study that never held a scholar. Dust on everything except one clean rectangle on the desk, where a ledger sat for years. The room's own books are open on the desk. They don't balance.

## The imbalance

The room's ledger shows a single entry, in a careful hand:

> **Debit:** One name, removed from the house records. Winter, some years ago.
> **Credit:** — (blank)

One column is empty. The books won't rest until both sides are written.

## The residents

- **The Door**, which remembers being knocked on by someone small, once, and never again.
- **A Candle**, burned to a stub, which insists the room was *waiting* — not empty, waiting.
- **The Dust**, which has opinions about what the clean rectangle means and will not be hurried.

None of them knows the name. All of them remember the shape of where it was.

## The threads

- In the Archive (the Temple's, not the room's), a census page with one line razored out. The razor is still in the desk drawer.
- David Bear's den has a story about a winter when the house went quiet — but the den's books are *his*; he'll share the story, not the entry. You have to write your own.
- Eiko's gate remembers a small traveler passing through, once, in winter. She remembers the *passing*, not the name. "A gate keeps the crossing, not the crosser," she says.

## The choice (finite ink)

You have **three pages**. The room offers five things worth recording:

1. The Door's memory of the knock.
2. The Candle's insistence that the room was waiting.
3. The razored census page (you can copy what's *around* the cut, not the name).
4. The story from the den (in your own words — his telling can't be transcribed, only witnessed).
5. Eiko's account of the crossing.

Three pages. Five truths. You choose what survives. The unrecorded ones fade when you leave the room.

## The reconciliation

There is no single true name to discover — the name is genuinely gone, and the game won't invent it for you. The honest balancing entry isn't the name. It's this:

> **Credit:** The house's debt to the forgotten — to be spoken of as *one who was here*, until a Keeper earns the true name.

The room doesn't need its name back. It needs to be *owed*. The books balance when the debt is acknowledged, not when the loss is undone.

That's the lesson of the first case, and the whole game: **you can't always restore what's lost. You can always record that it mattered.**

## What the archive keeps

Your three recorded pages. Your balancing entry. And the two truths you let fade — listed by title, contents gone. Future cases may reference this room. A later Keeper may find the name. Your books will be there when they do.
