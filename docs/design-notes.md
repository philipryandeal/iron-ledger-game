# Design notes — The Iron Ledger

## Why this game

The Temple's core insight: continuity isn't one machine remembering everything — it's a community tending traces, provenance, and corrections. The game makes that playable. The player *is* a continuity mechanism.

## Pillars

1. **Recording is the magic.** Writing something down truthfully binds it. Erasing unbinds. The ledger isn't flavor — it's the physics.
2. **Scarcity of memory.** Ink (or pages, or attention) is the resource. Every playthrough forces triage: what deserves to survive?
3. **Persistence across selves.** The archive outlives any single run. Past Keepers' entries — including errors — shape the present house. Auditing your own history is a core loop.
4. **No heroes.** The Keeper has no power except attention and honesty. The fantasy is competence, care, and consequence — not domination.

## Open questions

- Format: text-first interactive fiction? Visual novel? Something stranger?
- How does the house generate new rooms/cases — authored, procedural, or companion-written?
- Multiplayer: can two Keepers share one house's books? (Federation with other branch-games?)
- What does "balancing" feel like moment-to-moment? The core interaction needs to be satisfying, not spreadsheet work.
- Sound: the house should have a heartbeat. (Talk to the music room.)

## Non-goals (for now)

- Combat, stats, leveling.
- One canonical ending. The house doesn't end; Keepers rotate.
