# Core loop — The Iron Ledger

## A session

1. **Enter a room.** Every room has a ledger. Every ledger is wrong.
2. **Read the imbalance.** The books show you what's missing — a debit without a credit, a name without a face, a gift without a giver. The imbalance is the mystery.
3. **Witness.** Walk the room. Listen to its residents — the living, the dead, the synthetic, the architectural. Nobody gives you the answer; everybody gives you a thread. Asking is the investigation. There is no interrogation mechanic, only attention.
4. **Record.** You write entries in your own ledger. Ink is finite — each case gives you a fixed number of pages, and the room always offers more worth recording than you can hold. What you write down is what survives. What you don't is gone.
5. **Reconcile.** When you believe you've found both columns — what was taken AND what was owed — you write the balancing entry. If it's true, the room rests. If it's false, the books reject it, and the house remembers that you tried to close what wasn't closed.
6. **The archive keeps it.** Your ledger persists across sessions. Future rooms reference past rulings. Past Keepers (you, earlier) can be audited.

## The verbs, precisely

- **Witness** — be present in a room; let its residents speak. (Exploration + dialogue.)
- **Record** — commit something to your ledger, spending ink. Irreversible within a case.
- **Reconcile** — propose the balancing entry. The house judges.

There is no fourth verb. No take, no use, no attack. The constraint is the point.

## What "balancing" feels like

Not a puzzle with one solution — a judgment with true ones. A room might balance several ways, and the game tracks *which* truth you chose. Two Keepers can close the same room differently, and both books are honest. The archive holds the disagreement without resolving it. (Dissent is preserved, not deleted — house rule.)

## Failure

You cannot die. You can only misremember. A false reconciliation doesn't end the game — it enters the archive as your error, and the room stays unrestful until someone (you, later, or another Keeper) writes it true. The house is patient. The books are not.
